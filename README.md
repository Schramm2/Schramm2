I work in the practical layer around AI: agents, context, memory, internal tools, and the evaluation loops that tell you whether any of it is actually working.

I studied Computer Science and Information Systems at UCT and now build as a graduate AI engineer at [Ubundi](https://ubundi.com/). This account carries the whole arc, from early university projects through the full-stack years to the AI systems work I do now. The path matters as much as the destination.

The belief underneath all of it: **AI should amplify judgment, not replace it.** The interesting problems are rarely the model. They are context, trust, verification, and keeping a human accountable for the result.

## Notes to self

<details>
  <summary><b>How I think about AI</b></summary>

<br />

I do not want AI to replace the thinking part of my work. I want it to make the thinking more visible, more structured, and more ambitious. The best AI-assisted work still needs a human who knows what matters, notices when something is wrong, and takes responsibility for the final result.

</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>What Ubundi is teaching me</b></summary>

<br />

The hard part is rarely the model. It is context, trust, workflow design, observability, secure boundaries, and knowing when a human needs to stay in the loop. Production-adjacent AI engineering is mostly the engineering around the AI.

</details>
